Egypt fashion commerce: research map

Companies, people, numbers, relationships and facts pulled from the project’s research files, with conflicts and gaps shown rather than resolved.

Read first

This is a study map of the project’s own research files. It contains no new research. Every row points to the file(s) it came from, so you can go back and read the original. Nothing was changed or deleted.

How to read the tags

OfficialReportedCompany-reportedEstimateProjectedSurveyInterpretationConflictUnknown

‘Company-reported’ means the company said it and no independent measure was found. ‘Conflict’ means files disagree or a claim failed verification.

Source files

Short names in each row: ecommerce total e-commerce · fashion fashion market · companies · technology · ai · consumer · regulation · mena · final_report · fact_check · company_master.csv · funding_database.csv · partnership_database.csv. Where two files disagree, both are shown. Where a file gives no role, the row says ‘Not clearly specified’. Ownership and investment are listed only where a file states them.

6. Easy study summary

Start here if you want the short version.

Companies

  • Egyptian fashion commerce: TFK (curated marketplace), Gahez and Venduo (B2B supply chain), Libsy (Cairo resale), DressCode, Opio and Brantu (earlier brands/marketplaces), Mitcha (acquired, reported), Source Beauty (beauty-adjacent).
  • Big platforms with Egypt storefronts: Amazon, Noon, Jumia. The files show they sell fashion in Egypt but disclose no Egypt fashion revenue or share.
  • Regional and global AI vendors: Taffi/Amira, Get Outfit, Google, Max Fashion and about a dozen others. They appear as comparison, not as Egyptian deployments.
  • Institutions: the Central Bank of Egypt, CAPMAS, the tax, data-protection and consumer agencies supply most of the official numbers and rules.

People

  • Founders named: Fadi Antaki, Marianne Simaika, Karim Abd El Kader (TFK); Wael Olama and Mohamed El-Dahaby (Gahez); Khaled Solouma, Mohamed Mokhles, Ahmed Zidan (Venduo); Lara Soliman (Libsy); Shady Mokhtar and Reem Abdellaftif (Opio); Hilda Louca (Mitcha); Lydia Schoonderbeek (Source Beauty); Shahad Geoffrey and Pradeep Bisht (Taffi); Kim Sanzhiev (Get Outfit).
  • Executives: only where a title is given (e.g., Antaki, Solouma, Olama as CEO). Investors: Paul Antaki and Nasser Chourbagi appear as named TFK seed participants.
  • No person is documented as an owner or shareholder.

Money and numbers

  • Market size (do not blend): US$9.14bn (2023, est.), US$10.2bn (2024, est.), US$9.91bn B2C (2025), US$11.49bn GMV (2026, Mordor), US$2.342bn revenue (ECDB 2025). CBE: e-commerce payment value from EGP 29bn (2021) to over EGP 180bn (2024, expected).
  • Fashion: US$1.6bn apparel (2024); EGP 187.4bn clothing/footwear/accessories retail with EGP 11.0bn online (2025 forecast); US$401m fashion e-commerce revenue (ECDB 2025); US$820m vs US$4.5bn (conflict).
  • Funding: TFK US$2.6m (2022), Gahez US$2m (2022), Opio US$300,000, DressCode US$250,000. Namshi sold at US$335.2m.
  • Access: internet use 74.648% (2024); financial inclusion 74.8% (end-2024); 43.7m wallets (Q1 2025); only 8.3% regular online buyers (2025).
  • Prices: clothing and footwear inflation +12.6% year on year in Aug 2026.

Ownership and relationships

  • Investors: CVentures led TFK; Disruptech led Gahez; Egypt Ventures backed DressCode and Gahez; AUC Angels and Flat6Labs Cairo backed Opio.
  • Deals: Converted → Mitcha (2026); Noon → Namshi (2022–23; Emaar sold); Glamera → Bookr (MOU only).
  • Tech links: Syte–Shein; Google Cloud–Max Fashion (UAE). No verified partnership links an AI vendor to an Egyptian fashion company.
  • No ownership percentages exist for Egyptian companies.

Key facts

  • Egypt’s digital-payments base grew a lot between 2020 and 2025, but that is national data, not fashion.
  • The public startup record is thin after 2022; several companies have unknown status.
  • No Egyptian AI-core fashion company was found. Venduo’s ‘AI and Data Science’ claim is the strongest Egyptian AI statement, and it is company-claimed only.
  • The only 2027 items are forecasts.
